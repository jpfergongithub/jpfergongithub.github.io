I just found out that [Alvin Chang](https://www.vox.com/authors/alvin) over at Vox has put together [a really crackerjack article](https://www.vox.com/policy-and-politics/2019/2/18/18217346/work-home-segregation-map) on how much higher residential segregation is in America than employment segregation, and on how employment segregation has gotten worse over the last generation. I'm biased, because he cites [Rem Koning](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/profile.aspx?facId=876389)'s and my [*ASR* article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0003122418767438) from last year, but still.

What I find really impressive is that Alvin didn't get in touch with us before the video and article ran \(we're the support group here, alongside Hall, Iceland, and Yi's recent [*Population Research and Policy Review*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11113-019-09510-9) article \[gated\]\), but he *nailed* the interpretation of our findings. I thought I wrote the article as clearly as possible, but I've had trouble explaining its main import to reporters in the past.

The last thing that should be noted here is that, when they showed the paper in the video, they used the preprint version that Rem and I posted on [SocArXiv](https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/c6hsd/) before the article was accepted. The ease with which you can find it, versus the hoops I just jumped through to find the \(gated\) copy of Hall *et al.*'s article, is a pleasant little testament to the value of open access and preprint servers.

All in all, a good day. As long as you don't read the comments on the YouTube video, of course.
